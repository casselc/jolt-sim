## Finite `EINTR` retry policy

`eintr-retry-reference.smt2` fixes the implementation contract before the
writer is built. `max-eintr-retries` is the number of consecutive `EINTR`
results an active append may tolerate. `EINTR` advances no byte offset and
increments the count. The first `EINTR` that makes the count greater than the
allowance transitions to an absorbing failed state. Positive partial or full
write progress advances the byte offset and resets the consecutive count to
zero. Completion is possible only at the full frame length, and only that
active-to-complete transition advances `accepted-end`, once, by the full frame
length.

The reference counterexample query is UNSAT for frame lengths one through
eight, retry allowances zero through three, and five unrolled transitions.
Within that bound an all-`EINTR` trace cannot remain active, complete, advance
the byte position, or advance `accepted-end`. The latest possible exhaustion
occurs on transition four when the allowance is three; transition five observes
the absorbing failure.

`eintr-retry-buggy-accept.smt2` is SAT and witnesses the prohibited behavior:
with allowance two, three consecutive interruptions leave position zero but a
buggy exhaustion path reports complete and advances `accepted-end` from 100 to
108. `eintr-retry-boundary.smt2` is SAT and witnesses the intended reset rule:
two allowed interruptions, two bytes of progress, one fresh interruption, and
two final bytes complete a four-byte frame and advance `accepted-end` from 100
to 104 exactly once.

`eintr-ranking-reference.smt2` removes the retry-limit mismatch from the
bounded model. It quantifies symbolically over every mathematical integer
`max-eintr-retries >= 0` and every active retry count from zero through that
maximum. The positive natural rank
`max-eintr-retries + 1 - retries` decreases by exactly one on every active
`EINTR`; position and `accepted-end` remain unchanged. An active state at rank
one transitions to absorbing failure at rank zero. The negated one-step
inductive obligations are UNSAT, so natural-number well-foundedness rules out
an infinite all-`EINTR` active execution and covers a practical setting such as
64. The zero-through-three unroll remains the concrete trace companion.

## Review history

An initial independent Claude Sonnet read-only review rejected the first model
set because several reference files asserted their conclusion too directly,
length arithmetic used unbounded integers rather than wire-width arithmetic,
and corrupt headers were not modeled. Codex independently accepted those
findings and replaced the affected files with:

- unrolled cursor/count and halt/take scanner transitions;
- a two-execution journal-failure noninterference relation with named catch
  premises;
- `u32`-to-`u64` bit-vector arithmetic and a wrapped-`u32` buggy control;
- complete-header validation plus a bad-header-CRC control.

A second bounded Claude Sonnet review found no blocker in the revised models
and accepted the checkpoint with disclosure-only notes. Codex then added and
verified the partial-write/`EINTR` transition model and controls. External
review was advisory; the local Z3 gate above is the acceptance evidence.

## Required executable companions

Implementation remains blocked until its tests reproduce every SAT control and
boundary listed in `README.md`. In particular, the catch-policy premises,
CRC32C bytes, exact raw-tail reconstruction, repeated recovery determinism,
and byte-by-byte process crash cuts are not established by these SMT results.
The liveness conclusion uses the standard well-foundedness of natural numbers;
Z3 checks the local inductive and boundary obligations. Its arithmetic is
unbounded, so an implementation must prevent counter overflow or reject values
outside its representation. The concrete unroll is still limited to retry
allowances zero through three. The models treat outcome zero exclusively as
`EINTR`; zero-byte non-`EINTR` returns, other errors, cancellation, retry
delay/backoff, lock fairness, and real operating-system behavior remain
executable-test obligations.
